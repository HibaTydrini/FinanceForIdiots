# Johnson & Johnson (JNJ)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Health Care (pharmaceuticals and medical devices) | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE (USD)
Also known as: J&J, JNJ
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 28, 2025)

## What it does
Johnson & Johnson is one of the world's largest health care companies. Founded in 1887 and based in New Brunswick, New Jersey, it sells in virtually every country and had about 138,200 employees at the end of 2025. Its former consumer health business was split off into a separate company, Kenvue, in 2023, so J&J now has two segments:

- **Innovative Medicine** (prescription drugs): cancer, immunology, neuroscience, pulmonary hypertension, infectious diseases and cardiovascular. Key drugs include DARZALEX (multiple myeloma), STELARA and TREMFYA (immunology), XARELTO (blood thinner) and SPRAVATO (depression). 2025 sales: US$60.4 billion.
- **MedTech** (medical devices): heart devices, orthopaedics (joint replacements), surgery tools and vision products. 2025 sales: US$33.8 billion.

In October 2025 J&J announced it plans to separate its Orthopaedics business, targeted within 18 to 24 months.

## How it makes money
By selling patented medicines (which earn high profits while their patents last) and medical devices to hospitals, doctors and pharmacies around the world. About 43% of 2025 sales came from outside the U.S.

2025 results:
- Worldwide sales: US$94.2 billion, up 6.0%.
- Net earnings: US$26.8 billion (US$11.03 per share). This was much higher than 2024's US$14.1 billion, partly because of one-time items.
- The DARZALEX family alone was about 15% of revenue.
- Dividend: J&J has a very long record of annual dividend increases, which is why it is known as a dividend stock.

## What moves the stock
- Results of drug trials and approvals of new medicines.
- **Patent expiries.** When a big drug loses patent protection, cheaper copies (generics or "biosimilars") arrive and its sales drop.
- Drug pricing policy in the U.S.
- Legal developments, especially talc lawsuits.

## Main risks (from its annual report, in plain words)
1. **Talc lawsuits.** J&J faces many lawsuits claiming its talc-based baby powder caused illness. It does not buy outside product liability insurance, so large payouts could exceed what it has set aside.
2. **Patent expiries and biosimilars.** STELARA biosimilars are already reducing sales, and DARZALEX's main U.S. patents expire in 2029.
3. **U.S. drug price rules.** Under the Inflation Reduction Act, government-set prices for some drugs (including XARELTO and STELARA) begin in 2026.
4. **Regulation and investigations**, which can lead to fines or restrictions.
5. **Manufacturing and supply chain.** J&J runs 63 manufacturing sites, and disruptions can cause shortages.
6. **Need for new products.** Products launched in the last five years were about 25% of 2025 sales, and new drugs must keep replacing older ones.

## What this means for a beginner
J&J is a large, diversified, established company that is often considered "defensive": people need medicine in good and bad economies. It is popular with dividend investors. But even big, stable companies have specific risks. For J&J that means lawsuits and drugs losing patent protection. In a portfolio, it adds exposure to the Health Care sector.
