# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Information Technology (semiconductors) | Country: United States | Trades on: Nasdaq (USD)
Also known as: NVIDIA, Nvidia, NVDA
As of: fiscal year 2026 (ended January 25, 2026)

## What it does
NVIDIA designs computer chips called GPUs (graphics processing units) and the software and networking that go with them. It calls itself "a data center scale AI infrastructure company." Its chips are what most big tech companies use to train and run artificial intelligence (AI) models. It also makes GeForce graphics cards for video games and chips for professional workstations and cars.

NVIDIA designs the chips but does not manufacture them itself. Outside factories (foundries) such as TSMC and Samsung build them, mostly in Asia.

## How it makes money
NVIDIA reports two segments:
- **Compute & Networking**: Data Center chips, systems and AI software, plus Automotive.
- **Graphics**: GeForce gaming GPUs and RTX workstation GPUs.

Fiscal 2026 results:
- Total revenue: US$215.9 billion, up 65% from the year before.
- Data Center revenue: US$193.7 billion (up 68%), about 90% of all revenue.
- Gaming revenue: US$16.0 billion (up 41%).
- Net income (profit): about US$120.1 billion.
- Gross margin: 71.1%. NVIDIA keeps about 71 cents of every dollar of sales after the direct cost of making its products.
- Dividend: very small, US$0.01 per share per quarter. Most of the money goes back to shareholders through share buybacks.

Headquarters: Santa Clara, California. About 42,000 employees in 38 countries.

## What moves the stock
- **AI spending by big tech companies.** When cloud companies and AI labs announce bigger data center budgets, NVIDIA usually benefits. When investors worry those budgets will slow, the stock can fall quickly.
- **New chip launches**, such as the move from the Hopper to the Blackwell generation, and whether supply keeps up with demand.
- **U.S. export rules on China.** U.S. restrictions have effectively shut NVIDIA out of China's data center market. In fiscal 2026 it took a US$4.5 billion charge tied to its H20 chip for China.

## Main risks (from its annual report, in plain words)
1. **A few customers buy a large share.** One direct customer was 22% of fiscal 2026 revenue and another was 14%. If a big buyer cuts orders, sales can drop fast.
2. **Export controls and geopolitics.** More restrictions could block sales to more countries or products.
3. **Supply chain concentrated in Asia.** NVIDIA depends on outside foundries, and its supply chain is "mainly concentrated in Asia." Lead times can exceed 12 months.
4. **Competition.** Rivals, and big customers designing their own AI chips, could take market share or push prices down.
5. **Demand is hard to forecast.** Wrong guesses have caused both shortages and write-downs of excess inventory in the past.
6. **Product transitions.** Frequent new chip generations can cause gaps in demand and quality problems.

## What this means for a beginner
NVIDIA has been one of the biggest winners of the AI boom, and its profits are real and very large. But much of its value depends on AI spending continuing to grow. Its stock has historically moved a lot more than the overall market, both up and down. Having a large part of your account in one company like this means one piece of bad news (a customer cutting orders, a new export rule) can move your whole account. NVIDIA is also one of the largest holdings inside broad funds such as SPY and QQQ, so you may own more of it than you think.
