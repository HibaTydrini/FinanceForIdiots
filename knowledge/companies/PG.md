# The Procter & Gamble Company (PG)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Consumer Staples (household and personal care products) | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE (USD)
Also known as: P&G, Procter and Gamble, PG
As of: fiscal year 2026 (ended June 30, 2026)

## What it does
P&G makes everyday household and personal products sold in stores and online around the world. It was founded in Cincinnati, Ohio in 1837 and is still headquartered there. It had about 104,000 employees in mid-2026.

Its five segments (share of net sales) and major brands:
- **Fabric & Home Care (35%)**: Tide, Gain, Ariel, Downy, Dawn, Cascade, Febreze, Mr. Clean, Swiffer.
- **Baby, Feminine & Family Care (24%)**: Pampers, Luvs, Always, Tampax, plus paper products.
- **Beauty (19%)**: Head & Shoulders, Pantene, Herbal Essences, Olay, SK-II, Old Spice, Secret.
- **Health Care (14%)**: Crest, Oral-B, Vicks, Pepto-Bismol, Metamucil.
- **Grooming (8%)**: Gillette, Venus, Braun.

## How it makes money
By selling a lot of low-priced products that people buy again and again, mostly through retailers. Walmart alone was about 16% of sales, and its top ten customers were about 43%.

Fiscal 2026 results:
- Net sales: US$87.0 billion, up 3%.
- Net earnings: about US$16.0 billion.
- Gross margin: 50.2% of sales, down 1 percentage point.
- Dividend: P&G has paid a dividend for **136 consecutive years** and increased it for **70 consecutive years** (since 1956).

## What moves the stock
- Sales growth, from both selling more units and raising prices.
- Costs of raw materials, many of which are petroleum-based, plus packaging and transport.
- Currency exchange rates, since a lot of sales are outside the U.S.
- Interest rates. Like other steady dividend stocks, it can fall when rates rise.

## Main risks (from its annual report, in plain words)
1. **Cost swings.** Commodity, energy and transport costs can rise faster than P&G can raise prices.
2. **International and currency risk** from operating in many countries.
3. **Dependence on big retailers** like Walmart, and competition from cheaper store brands.
4. **Supply chain disruptions.**
5. **Reputation of its brands.** Product quality problems can hurt trust.
6. **Economic and geopolitical uncertainty** that changes what consumers buy.

## What this means for a beginner
P&G is a classic "defensive" or "consumer staples" stock: people keep buying toothpaste, detergent and diapers even in a recession. That usually makes it steadier than the overall market, and its dividend record is one of the longest anywhere. The trade-off is slower growth, since sales grew 3% in fiscal 2026. Defensive stocks like P&G can help balance a portfolio that also holds riskier growth stocks.
