# iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond ETF (AGG)

Type: Fund (ETF, bonds) | Focus: the whole U.S. investment-grade bond market | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE Arca (USD)
Also known as: AGG, iShares Core U.S. Aggregate Bond
As of: November 12–13, 2026 (fund page)

## What it is
AGG holds a very broad mix of U.S. bonds. It tracks the **Bloomberg US Aggregate Bond Index** ("the Agg"), a common benchmark for the U.S. bond market. It "seeks to track an index that includes U.S. investment-grade bonds." It launched September 22, 2003.

## Key facts (as of Nov 2026)
- Holdings: 13,522 bonds.
- Expense ratio: 0.03% per year (US$0.30 per US$1,000 invested).
- Net assets: about US$137.0 billion.
- Effective duration: 5.70 years.
- 30-day SEC yield: 5.03%. 12-month trailing yield: 4.18%.

What's inside (share of the fund):
- U.S. Treasury (government) bonds: 46.69%
- Mortgage-backed securities (pools of home mortgages): 23.00%
- Corporate bonds (industrial, financial and utility companies): about 25%
- Other (sovereign, agency, local authority, asset-backed): the rest

Credit quality: about 88% rated AAA, AA or A; 11.68% BBB; 0% below investment grade.

## Why bond prices can fall (duration)
When interest rates rise, existing bonds lose value. With a duration of about 5.7 years, a 1 percentage point rise in rates would lower AGG's price by roughly 5.7%. That is much less than a long-term bond fund like TLT (duration about 14.75 years).

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Interest rate risk**, moderate for AGG.
2. **Credit risk** is low because everything is investment grade, but corporate bonds can weaken in a recession.
3. **Inflation** reduces the real value of interest payments.
4. **Currency risk for Canadians.** Priced in U.S. dollars.

## What this means for a beginner
AGG is a very low-cost, broadly diversified bond fund. Bonds like these are often used to make a portfolio steadier: they usually move much less than stocks and pay regular interest. They can still lose value when interest rates jump, as in 2022, but much less than long-term bond funds.
