# The Toronto-Dominion Bank (TD.TO / TD)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Financials (bank) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (TD, CAD) and NYSE (TD, USD)
Also known as: TD Bank, TD Bank Group, TD
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended October 31, 2025)

## What it does
TD is one of Canada's largest banks, and it also owns a large retail bank in the U.S. It is the second-largest holding in the S&P/TSX 60 index, at about 6.9% of XIU as of October 2026.

Its business segments:
- **Canadian Personal and Commercial Banking**: everyday banking, mortgages, cards and business loans in Canada.
- **U.S. Retail**: TD's U.S. banking operations.
- **Wealth Management and Insurance**: investing, financial advice and insurance. Wealth assets passed a record C$1.3 trillion.
- **Wholesale Banking**: TD Securities, investment banking and trading.

## How it makes money
Mostly from interest on loans and mortgages minus interest paid on deposits, plus fees from wealth management, insurance and investment banking.

Fiscal 2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Reported net income: C$20.5 billion. This was boosted by a one-time gain of about C$8.6 billion from selling its whole stake in the U.S. broker Charles Schwab in February 2025.
- Adjusted net income (excluding one-time items): C$15.0 billion.
- Dividends: C$4.20 per share for the year (C$1.05 per quarter).

## The U.S. anti-money-laundering problem
This is the biggest company-specific issue at TD. In October 2024, TD reached a "Global Resolution" with U.S. authorities over failures in its U.S. anti-money-laundering (AML) controls and paid US$3.088 billion. As part of it:
- TD's U.S. bank is **not allowed to grow its total assets above US$434 billion** (an asset cap) until regulators are satisfied. It had US$382 billion at October 31, 2025.
- TD has completed most of its fixes, but says "significant work and important milestones remain" through calendar 2027. It expects about US$500 million in U.S. AML remediation costs in fiscal 2026.

## What moves the stock
- Progress (or setbacks) on the U.S. AML remediation and the asset cap.
- The Canadian and U.S. economies, housing and loan losses.
- Interest rates.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Regulatory and legal.** The U.S. asset cap limits growth, and further problems could bring more penalties or restrictions.
2. **Credit risk**: more borrowers failing to repay in a downturn.
3. **Housing market** exposure through mortgages.
4. **Interest rate changes** affecting lending margins.
5. **Cyber and technology risk.**

## What this means for a beginner
TD is a large, long-established bank and a popular dividend stock in Canada. Its 2025 profit looks unusually high because of the one-time Schwab sale, so it's better to look at adjusted earnings. Its U.S. growth is limited until it finishes fixing its AML problems. If you own TD alongside other banks and insurers, much of your account depends on the financial sector.
