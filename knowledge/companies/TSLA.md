# Tesla, Inc. (TSLA)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Consumer Discretionary (electric vehicles, energy storage) | Country: United States | Trades on: Nasdaq (USD)
Also known as: Tesla, TSLA
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Tesla designs, builds and sells electric cars (Model 3, Model Y and others). It also sells battery energy storage: Powerwall for homes and Megapack for utilities and large businesses. More and more, Tesla describes itself as an AI and robotics company. It sells "FSD (Supervised)" driver-assistance software, launched a Robotaxi ride-hailing service in June 2025, and is developing Cybercab (a purpose-built self-driving car) and Optimus (a humanoid robot).

Headquarters: Austin, Texas. Employees: 134,785 at the end of 2025.

## How it makes money
Tesla reports two segments:
- **Automotive & services and other**: selling and leasing cars, regulatory credits (sold to other carmakers that need them to meet emissions rules), service, Supercharging and software. US$82.1 billion in 2025.
  - Within that, total automotive revenue was US$69.5 billion, down 10% from 2024.
  - Regulatory credits brought in about US$2.0 billion (down from US$2.8 billion in 2024). This income depends on emissions rules and can shrink if they change.
- **Energy generation and storage**: US$12.8 billion in 2025, up 27%, driven by more Megapack and Powerwall deployments.

2025 results:
- Total revenue: US$94.8 billion, down 3% from US$97.7 billion in 2024.
- Net income: about US$3.8 billion.
- Vehicles: about 1.66 million produced and 1.64 million delivered.
- Dividend: none.

## What moves the stock
- Quarterly delivery numbers and car prices (price cuts help sales but hurt profit margins).
- News about self-driving, Robotaxi and Optimus. A large part of Tesla's value is based on these *future* businesses, not today's car sales.
- Elon Musk: his decisions, statements and other companies often move the stock.
- Competition from other electric carmakers, especially in China and Europe.
- Interest rates, since many cars are bought with loans.

## Main risks (from its annual report, in plain words)
1. **Dependence on Elon Musk.** The annual report says Tesla is "highly dependent on the services of Elon Musk," who also leads other companies.
2. **Self-driving and robots may take longer or cost more** than expected, and need regulators to approve them. Laws were written assuming a human driver.
3. **Competition** in electric vehicles is growing and is pushing prices down.
4. **Demand for cars** depends on the economy, interest rates and government incentives.
5. **Production and supply chain.** New products and factories can be delayed, and batteries depend on raw materials.
6. **Safety and legal issues.** Collisions involving Autopilot, FSD or Robotaxi vehicles can lead to lawsuits, investigations and recalls.

## What this means for a beginner
Tesla is a famous company, but its stock is much more volatile (bumpy) than the market average. Its price reflects big hopes for future businesses like Robotaxi and robots, so it can swing sharply on news. In 2025 its car revenue actually fell while energy storage grew. If you buy Tesla, expect big moves in both directions. It's usually wise to keep it a small part of a diversified account. Tesla is also inside broad funds like SPY and QQQ.
