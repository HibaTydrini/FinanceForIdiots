# Manulife Financial Corporation (MFC.TO / MFC)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Financials (life insurance and wealth management) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (MFC, CAD) and NYSE (MFC, USD)
Also known as: Manulife, MFC (operates as John Hancock in the United States)
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Manulife is a large Canadian insurance and investment company, based in Toronto. It sells life and health insurance, and manages savings and retirement money for individuals and companies.

Its four segments:
- **Asia**: insurance and wealth products in Asian markets, its fastest-growing region. Core earnings grew 18% in 2025.
- **Canada**: insurance and wealth products for Canadians.
- **U.S.**: life insurance (under the John Hancock name).
- **Global Wealth and Asset Management (Global WAM)**: mutual funds, retirement plans and institutional money management, with about C$1,071 billion in average assets under management and administration in 2025.

## How it makes money
- **Insurance**: it collects premiums today and pays claims later. Meanwhile it invests that money, mostly in bonds, and earns investment income.
- **Wealth and asset management**: it charges fees based on how much money it manages.

2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Core earnings (Manulife's main measure, excluding market swings): a record C$7.5 billion, up 3% at constant exchange rates.
- Net income attributed to shareholders: about C$5.6 billion.
- Core earnings per share: C$4.21, up 8%.
- Dividend: raised by 10.2%.

## What moves the stock
- Interest rates and the bond market. Insurers invest heavily in bonds, and rates affect what they earn.
- Stock market levels, which change the fees earned on money managed.
- Growth in Asia.
- Insurance claims experience (for example, people living longer or getting sick more often than expected).

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Market and interest rate risk.** Falling markets reduce fee income, and big rate moves can affect results.
2. **Insurance risk.** Claims can be higher than expected.
3. **Asia exposure.** Economic or political problems in Asia would hurt its growth engine.
4. **Regulation and accounting rules** for insurers can change.

## What this means for a beginner
Manulife is a big, established insurer that pays a dividend. Life insurers are financial companies, just like banks. If you own Manulife together with Canadian banks such as RBC and TD, a large part of your account is in one sector (Financials), so a bad period for the financial sector would hit several of your holdings at once.
