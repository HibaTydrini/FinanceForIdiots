# Bitcoin (BTC-USD)

Type: Crypto asset (not a company) | Category: Cryptocurrency | Priced in: U.S. dollars (BTC-USD)
Also known as: Bitcoin, BTC, BTC-USD
As of: November 2026

## What it is
Bitcoin is a digital money and payment network with no central authority, often described as "cash for the Internet." It is **not a company**: there is no CEO, no profits and no dividends. Nobody owns the network. Developers can propose changes, but they only take effect if the users, miners and computers running the network adopt them.

Key features:
- **Fixed supply.** Only 21 million bitcoins will ever exist. Each bitcoin can be split into 100,000,000 small units called satoshis.
- **Mining.** New bitcoins are created as a reward to "miners," who use specialized computers to process transactions and secure the network. This reward is cut in half on a set schedule (the "halving") until new issuance eventually stops.
- **Public record.** All transactions are recorded on a public ledger (the blockchain), so Bitcoin is not anonymous.

## What gives it value / what moves the price
Bitcoin's price is set purely by **supply and demand**: what buyers are willing to pay. Things that tend to move it:
- Investor enthusiasm or fear (it often moves with "risk-on" assets like tech stocks).
- Interest rates and the general appetite for risk.
- Regulation and news about crypto platforms.
- Large buyers or sellers. Bitcoin.org notes that modest amounts of money can move the price a lot.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Extreme volatility.** Bitcoin can lose more than half of its value in a year. Bitcoin.org itself says there is "no guarantee" it keeps its value and that it could become worthless.
2. **No income.** It pays no interest or dividends, so your whole return depends on the price.
3. **Platform risk.** Canadian securities regulators warn that unregistered crypto trading platforms "may not have essential safeguards" to protect your assets from loss, theft or misuse, and that even registration "cannot eliminate all risks."
4. **No deposit insurance.** Crypto held on a platform is not protected like a bank deposit.
5. **Irreversible mistakes.** Sent bitcoins generally can't be recovered, and a lost wallet means lost coins.
6. **Hacking and scams** are common in the crypto world.

## How Canadians can hold it
- Through a crypto trading platform registered with Canadian securities regulators. You can check registration with the Canadian Securities Administrators (CSA) national registration search.
- Through a Bitcoin ETF on the Toronto Stock Exchange, which can be held in a regular brokerage account, TFSA or RRSP.

## What this means for a beginner
Canadian regulators say crypto trading comes with "elevated levels of risk that may not be suitable for many investors, in particular retail investors." Many people who own Bitcoin keep it to a small slice of their account, an amount they could afford to see fall by half or more without changing their plans. If crypto is a large part of your account, or you need the money soon, a single bad crypto year could seriously set you back.
