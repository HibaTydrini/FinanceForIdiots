# SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY)

Type: Fund (ETF, stocks) | Focus: S&P 500 index, about 500 of the largest U.S. companies | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE Arca (USD)
Also known as: SPY, SPDR S&P 500, "Spider", State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust
As of: November 12, 2026 (fund page)

## What it is
SPY is the oldest and largest U.S.-listed ETF. It tracks the **S&P 500 Index**, about 500 of the largest publicly traded U.S. companies across all sectors. It launched January 22, 1993, is managed by State Street, and is legally structured as a "unit investment trust."

## Key facts (as of Nov 12, 2026)
- Holdings: 504 stocks (some companies have two share classes).
- Expense ratio: 0.0945% per year, under US$1 for every US$1,000 invested.
- Assets: about US$807 billion.
- Largest sector: Information Technology at 36.15%.
- Top 10 holdings: Apple 7.90%, NVIDIA 6.14%, Microsoft 5.93%, Amazon 4.01%, Alphabet (Class A) 2.68%, Broadcom 2.65%, Alphabet (Class C) 2.14%, Meta Platforms 2.10%, Eli Lilly 1.74%, Tesla 1.71%.

## What moves it
- The overall U.S. economy and corporate profits.
- The biggest companies. Because the index is weighted by size, the top 10 make up a large share.
- Interest rates and inflation.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Stock market risk.** In a bear market, the S&P 500 can fall 20% or more.
2. **Growing tech concentration.** Technology is now more than a third of the fund, so it is less spread out than it used to be.
3. **Only the U.S.** It doesn't hold Canadian or international companies.
4. **Currency risk for Canadians.** It is priced in U.S. dollars.

## What this means for a beginner
SPY is one of the simplest ways to own a slice of the whole U.S. stock market at a very low cost. It is much more diversified than owning a few individual stocks. It still goes down when the market goes down, and it is still all stocks, so it can drop a lot in a bad year. If you also own QQQ or big tech stocks like NVIDIA, note that SPY already holds those same companies, so you may be more concentrated in tech than you think.
