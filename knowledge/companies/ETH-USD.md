# Ethereum / Ether (ETH-USD)

Type: Crypto asset (not a company) | Category: Cryptocurrency / smart-contract platform | Priced in: U.S. dollars (ETH-USD)
Also known as: Ethereum, Ether, ETH, ETH-USD
As of: November 2026

## What it is
Ethereum is a public blockchain, a shared global computer network run by thousands of independent computers. Its own cryptocurrency is called **ether (ETH)**. It launched in July 2015 (co-founded by Vitalik Buterin and others). It is **not a company**: there are no profits, dividends or CEO.

Unlike Bitcoin, which is mainly digital money, Ethereum lets people run programs called **smart contracts**. These power applications such as:
- Stablecoins (crypto tokens meant to track a currency like the U.S. dollar)
- Decentralized finance ("DeFi"): lending and trading without a bank
- NFTs (digital collectibles), games and social apps

## How ETH is used
- **Fees ("gas")**: every transaction or app action on Ethereum costs a small fee paid in ETH. Part of each fee is permanently destroyed ("burned").
- **Staking**: since "The Merge" in 2022, Ethereum is secured by "proof of stake." Validators lock up ETH as a security deposit and earn more ETH for honest work, but lose part of it if they cheat. This switch cut Ethereum's energy use by about 99%, according to ethereum.org.

## What moves the price
- Supply and demand for ETH, driven by investor enthusiasm or fear.
- How much the network is used (more use means more fees paid in ETH).
- Interest rates and overall appetite for risk.
- Regulation and news about crypto platforms.
- It often moves together with Bitcoin, and often even more sharply.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Extreme volatility.** Ether can lose more than half of its value in a year.
2. **No income from the asset itself.** Staking rewards exist, but they come with their own platform and technical risks.
3. **Smart contract bugs and hacks.** In 2016, a flaw in one Ethereum application (The DAO) let attackers drain about US$60 million worth of ETH.
4. **High fees** at busy times.
5. **Platform risk.** Canadian regulators warn that unregistered crypto platforms "may not have essential safeguards" to protect your assets, and that registration "cannot eliminate all risks."
6. **No deposit insurance**, and mistakes or lost keys are usually irreversible.

## What this means for a beginner
Ethereum is the second-best-known crypto asset and has real uses as a platform for apps. But as an investment it is speculative: its price swings even more than many tech stocks, and Canadian regulators say crypto may not be suitable for many retail investors. If you hold it, many people keep it to a small slice of their account. Owning both Bitcoin and Ether does not spread your risk much, because they tend to rise and fall together.
