# SPDR Gold Shares (GLD)

Type: Fund (exchange-traded commodity fund, physical gold) | Focus: the price of gold | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE Arca (USD)
Also known as: GLD, SPDR Gold Trust, SPDR Gold Shares
As of: November 2026 (fund page)

## What it is
GLD is a trust that holds **physical gold bars**. Each share represents a slice of that gold. Its shares are designed to reflect the price of gold bullion, minus the fund's expenses, measured against the LBMA Gold Price PM (a daily benchmark gold price). The gold is held by two custodians, JPMorgan Chase Bank and HSBC Bank, and must be held in "allocated" form (specific identified bars). GLD launched November 18, 2004.

## Key facts
- Expense ratio: 0.40% per year (US$4 per US$1,000 invested), higher than most stock and bond index ETFs.
- Pays no dividends or interest. Gold itself produces no income.

## What moves it
- **The price of gold**, which depends on:
  - Fear and uncertainty (wars, financial crises). Gold is often seen as a "safe haven."
  - Interest rates. Gold pays no interest, so it tends to be less attractive when rates are high.
  - The U.S. dollar, since gold is priced in U.S. dollars.
  - Buying by central banks and investors.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Price swings.** Gold can rise or fall significantly for long periods.
2. **No income.** All of your return depends on the gold price going up.
3. **Fees** slowly reduce your holdings over time.
4. **Currency risk for Canadians.** Priced in U.S. dollars.
5. **Tax rules** for gold funds can differ from regular stock funds. GLD's page urges investors to consult a tax advisor.

## What this means for a beginner
Gold behaves differently from stocks and bonds, so a small amount can help diversify a portfolio, especially during crises. But it doesn't earn interest or profits the way bonds and companies do, so it isn't a growth engine. Most investors who own gold keep it to a small slice of their account.
