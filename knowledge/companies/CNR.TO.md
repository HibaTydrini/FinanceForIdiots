# Canadian National Railway Company (CNR.TO / CNI)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Industrials (railroad) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (CNR, CAD) and NYSE (CNI, USD)
Also known as: CN, CN Rail, Canadian National, CNR, CNI
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
CN runs a freight railway, moving goods (not passengers) by train. Its network is nearly 20,000 miles long (18,900 route miles) and connects Canada's East and West coasts with the U.S. Midwest and the U.S. Gulf Coast. It moves more than 300 million tons of goods a year and has served North America since 1919. Headquarters: Montreal, Quebec.

## How it makes money
It charges customers to move their goods. 2025 freight revenue by type of goods (Canadian dollars):
- Intermodal (shipping containers and trailers): C$3.9 billion
- Grain and fertilizers: C$3.7 billion
- Petroleum and chemicals: C$3.5 billion
- Metals and minerals: C$2.0 billion
- Forest products (lumber, pulp, paper): C$1.8 billion
- Coal: C$1.0 billion
- Automotive: C$0.9 billion

2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Total revenues: C$17.3 billion, up 2%.
- Net income: C$4.7 billion, up 6%.
- Diluted earnings per share: C$7.57, up 8%.
- Operating ratio: 61.9%. This is a railroad efficiency measure: operating costs were 61.9 cents for each dollar of revenue, and lower is better.
- Dividend: C$3.55 per share in 2025. A 3% increase for 2026 makes it the 30th consecutive year of increases.

## What moves the stock
- How much freight the economy ships: grain harvests, energy, consumer goods and imports through ports.
- **Trade and tariffs.** CN carries a lot of goods between Canada and the U.S. and to ports, so trade tensions and tariffs can reduce volumes. It cites "global trade tensions" as a heightened risk, and forest products revenue fell 5% in 2025.
- Efficiency (operating ratio) and fuel costs.
- Weather (harsh winters, floods, wildfires) and labour disruptions.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Economic slowdowns** reduce how much freight is shipped.
2. **Trade barriers and tariffs** between Canada, the U.S. and other countries.
3. **Weather and natural disasters** that disrupt the network.
4. **Labour disruptions** (strikes or lockouts).
5. **Regulation and safety**: accidents, especially involving dangerous goods, can be very costly.

## What this means for a beginner
Railways are often seen as solid long-term businesses: it is very hard for a competitor to build a new cross-country railway. CN has a long record of raising its dividend. But its results follow the economy and trade, so it can have weaker years when shipping volumes drop. In a portfolio, CN adds exposure to the Industrials sector, which helps spread your money beyond banks and energy.
