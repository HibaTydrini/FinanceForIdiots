# iShares Core Canadian Universe Bond Index ETF (XBB.TO)

Type: Fund (ETF, bonds) | Focus: Canadian investment-grade bonds (government and corporate) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (XBB, CAD)
Also known as: XBB, iShares Canadian Universe Bond
As of: October 31, 2026 (fund fact sheet)

## What it is
XBB holds a large basket of Canadian bonds. A bond is a loan to a government or company that pays you interest. XBB tracks the **FTSE Canada Universe Bond Index**, which covers federal government, provincial, municipal and corporate bonds rated investment grade. It aims "to provide income" and is designed as a long-term core holding. It launched November 20, 2000.

## Key facts (as of Oct 31, 2026)
- Holdings: 1,934 bonds.
- Management fee: 0.09%. MER (total yearly cost): 0.10%.
- Net assets: about C$11.0 billion.
- Distributions: monthly.
- Effective duration: 6.68 years (see below). Average maturity: 9.43 years.

- Can be held in a TFSA, RRSP and other registered accounts.

## Why bond prices can fall (duration)
When interest rates rise, newly issued bonds pay more, so older bonds paying less interest become worth less, and their prices drop. **Duration** estimates how much: with a duration of about 6.7 years, a 1 percentage point rise in interest rates would lower XBB's price by roughly 6.7%. That is what happened in 2022: rates rose quickly and XBB lost about 12% that year. The upside is that after rates rise, the fund earns higher interest going forward.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Interest rate risk.** Rising rates lower bond prices, as shown in 2022.
2. **Credit risk.** A small risk that some issuers can't pay. It's low here because the bonds are investment grade.
3. **Inflation** reduces the real value of fixed interest payments.

## What this means for a beginner
XBB is a simple, very low-cost way to own the whole Canadian bond market. Bonds usually move much less than stocks and often help when stocks fall, which is why many portfolios combine the two. But "safe" doesn't mean the price can never drop: 2022 showed bond funds can lose money too, especially when interest rates rise quickly. If you bought around 2020–2021, you may still be below your purchase price even though the fund keeps paying interest.
