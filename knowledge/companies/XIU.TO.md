# iShares S&P/TSX 60 Index ETF (XIU.TO)

Type: Fund (ETF, stocks) | Focus: S&P/TSX 60 index, 60 of the largest Canadian companies | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (XIU, CAD)
Also known as: XIU, iShares TSX 60, TSX 60 ETF
As of: October 31, 2026 (fund fact sheet)

## What it is
XIU holds the 60 large Canadian companies in the **S&P/TSX 60 Index**. BlackRock calls it "the largest and most liquid ETF in Canada." It "started trading in 1990, making it the first ETF in the world." Its current fund launch date is listed as September 28, 1999.

## Key facts (as of Oct 31, 2026)
- Holdings: 61.
- Management fee: 0.15%. MER (total yearly cost): 0.17%.
- Net assets: about C$23.2 billion.
- Distributions (payouts): quarterly. Distribution yield: 2.13%.
- Can be held in a TFSA, RRSP and other registered accounts.
- Top 10 holdings (48% of the fund): Royal Bank of Canada 9.53%, TD 6.93%, Shopify 6.45%, Bank of Montreal 4.06%, Canadian Natural Resources 3.91%, Bank of Nova Scotia 3.87%, Enbridge 3.72%, CIBC 3.60%, Suncor Energy 3.15%, Brookfield 2.84%.

## Past returns (calendar years, in CAD)
2021: +27.90% | 2022: −6.36% | 2023: +11.82% | 2024: +20.77% | 2025: +28.89%
Past performance does not predict future returns.

## What moves it
- The Canadian economy, especially **banks** and **energy**, the two biggest groups in the index.
- Oil and gas prices.
- Interest rates and the housing market (through the banks).

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Concentrated in a few sectors.** Five big banks alone are about 28% of the fund, and energy companies are also a large share.
2. **Only Canada.** Canada is a small part of the world's stock market, so many industries (like large tech and health care companies) are under-represented.
3. **Stock market risk.** It can fall significantly in a bad year.

## What this means for a beginner
XIU is a low-cost, simple way to own Canada's biggest companies in one purchase. It is far more diversified than owning one or two stocks. Many Canadians use it as a core holding. Because it leans on banks and energy and only holds Canadian companies, it is common to pair it with U.S. or international funds and bonds. If you already own big banks, Shopify or energy stocks directly, XIU holds them too, so you're doubling up.
