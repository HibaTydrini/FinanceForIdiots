# Fortis Inc. (FTS.TO / FTS)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Utilities (regulated electric and gas utilities) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (FTS, CAD) and NYSE (FTS, USD)
Also known as: Fortis, FTS
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Fortis owns electric and natural gas utilities, the companies that deliver power and gas to homes and businesses. It describes itself as "a diversified leader in the North American regulated electric and gas utility industry." About 95% of its assets are wires, pipes and transmission lines that deliver energy, rather than power plants. Headquarters: St. John's, Newfoundland and Labrador.

- About 3.5 million customers in five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states and the Caribbean.
- 67% of its assets are outside Canada.
- 9,900 employees. 2025 revenue: about C$12 billion. Total assets: about C$75 billion.

Its main utilities include:
- **ITC**: electric transmission lines in Michigan and seven other U.S. states.
- **UNS Energy (Tucson Electric Power)**: electricity and gas in Arizona.
- **Central Hudson**: electricity and gas in New York's Mid-Hudson Valley.
- **FortisBC**: the largest natural gas distributor in British Columbia.
- **FortisAlberta**: electricity distribution in Alberta.
- **Newfoundland Power** and **Caribbean Utilities** (Grand Cayman).

## How it makes money
"Virtually all" of Fortis's assets are **regulated**. Government regulators set the rates its utilities can charge, allowing them to recover costs and earn an approved return on the money they invest. When Fortis invests more in its networks (its "rate base"), it can usually earn more.

2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Net earnings: about C$1.7 billion (C$3.40 per share).
- Dividends paid: C$2.49 per share in 2025, up 4%.
- In late 2025 it raised its quarterly dividend 4.1% to C$0.64, its **52nd consecutive year** of dividend increases.
- Plan: invest C$28.8 billion from 2026 to 2030, growing its rate base from C$42.4 billion to C$57.9 billion, and targets 4% to 6% dividend growth per year through 2030.

## What moves the stock
- **Interest rates.** Utilities are often bought for steady dividends, so they tend to fall when rates rise (bonds become more attractive) and rise when rates fall.
- Regulators' decisions on rates and allowed returns.
- Progress on its capital investment plan.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Regulation.** Earnings depend on regulators' decisions. In 2024, a U.S. regulator (FERC) cut ITC's allowed return, reducing earnings by C$20 million.
2. **Interest rates** affect both its borrowing costs and how attractive its dividend looks.
3. **Weather.** Milder weather reduced electricity sales in Arizona in 2025, and earnings vary by season.
4. **Funding a large investment plan** requires steady access to debt and equity markets.

## What this means for a beginner
Fortis is considered a "defensive" stock. Because people need electricity and heat no matter what the economy does, its earnings tend to be steadier than most companies'. It has one of the longest dividend-growth records in Canada. Steadier does not mean risk-free: its price can still fall, especially when interest rates rise. It also grows more slowly than tech companies.
