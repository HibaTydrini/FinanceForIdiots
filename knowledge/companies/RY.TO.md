# Royal Bank of Canada (RY.TO / RY)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Financials (bank) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (RY, CAD) and NYSE (RY, USD)
Also known as: RBC, Royal Bank, RY
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended October 31, 2025)

## What it does
RBC is Canada's largest bank. It is the largest holding in the S&P/TSX 60 index, at about 9.5% of XIU as of October 2026. It serves "19+ million clients" and had 96,628 full-time-equivalent employees at the end of fiscal 2025.

Its five business segments:
- **Personal Banking**: chequing and savings accounts, mortgages, credit cards and loans for individuals.
- **Commercial Banking**: loans and banking for businesses.
- **Wealth Management**: investment advice, asset management and private banking, in Canada and the U.S.
- **Insurance**: life, health and other insurance.
- **Capital Markets**: investment banking and trading for large companies, governments and institutions.

In March 2024 RBC bought HSBC Bank Canada, which made it even bigger in Canadian banking.

## How it makes money
- **Net interest income**: the difference between the interest it earns on loans and mortgages and the interest it pays on deposits.
- **Fees**: from wealth management, investment banking, trading, insurance and account services.

Fiscal 2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Net income: about C$20.4 billion, up 25%.
- Diluted earnings per share: C$14.07, up 25%.
- Provisions for credit losses (money set aside for loans that may not be repaid): C$4.36 billion, up from C$3.23 billion.
- Dividend: C$1.64 per share per quarter, raised by 6%.

## What moves the stock
- The Canadian economy, the housing market and unemployment. These affect how many borrowers repay their loans.
- Interest rates. They change how much the bank earns on lending.
- Capital markets activity (deals, trading), which boosts fee income in good years.
- Loan losses. Rising provisions are an early warning sign that investors watch closely.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Credit risk.** If the economy weakens, more people and businesses can't repay loans. RBC's provisions rose in 2025.
2. **Housing market.** Canadian banks have large mortgage books.
3. **Interest rate changes** can squeeze the bank's lending margin.
4. **Regulation.** Banks must hold extra capital, and rules can change.
5. **Cyber attacks and technology failures.**

## What this means for a beginner
RBC is a large, established bank and a common dividend stock in Canada. Banks are a big part of the Canadian stock market: in XIU, RBC, TD, Bank of Montreal, Scotiabank and CIBC together make up about 28% of the fund (October 2026). If you own several Canadian banks and insurers at once, a big share of your account depends on the same thing: the health of the Canadian economy and housing market. That is called sector concentration.
