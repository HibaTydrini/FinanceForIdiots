# Invesco QQQ Trust (QQQ)

Type: Fund (ETF, stocks) | Focus: Nasdaq-100 index, large U.S. companies with a heavy tilt to technology | Country: United States | Trades on: Nasdaq (USD)
Also known as: QQQ, "the Qs", Invesco QQQ, Nasdaq-100 ETF
As of: November 2026 (fund page)

## What it is
QQQ is an exchange-traded fund (ETF), a basket of stocks you can buy in one trade. It tracks the **Nasdaq-100 Index**, which "comprises the 100 largest non-financial companies traded on the Nasdaq." Because the Nasdaq is where most big U.S. tech companies are listed, QQQ is dominated by large technology and tech-related companies. It is one of the oldest and most traded ETFs, launched March 10, 1999. The sponsor is Invesco Capital Management LLC.

## Key facts
- Index: Nasdaq-100.
- Holdings: about 100 companies.
- Expense ratio (yearly fee): 0.18%, which is US$1.80 per year for every US$1,000 invested.
- Banks and other financial companies are excluded by design.
- The index is weighted by company size, so the biggest companies make up a large share of the fund.

## What moves it
- The share prices of the largest U.S. tech companies, which make up a big part of the fund.
- Investor views on technology and AI growth.
- Interest rates. Fast-growing tech companies are especially sensitive to rate changes.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Concentration in tech.** Even though it holds about 100 companies, they lean heavily on the same sector. When tech falls, most of QQQ falls together.
2. **Top-heavy.** A handful of very large companies drive much of the return.
3. **More volatile than a broad market fund** like SPY, because it excludes steadier sectors such as banks.
4. **Currency risk for Canadians.** QQQ is priced in U.S. dollars, so changes in the USD/CAD exchange rate also change its value in Canadian dollars.

## What this means for a beginner
QQQ is a simple, low-cost way to own many big tech-focused companies at once, which is better than betting on a single tech stock. But "many companies" does not automatically mean "well diversified." If QQQ is most of your account, your account mostly depends on how U.S. tech does. QQQ has done very well in tech booms and fallen hard in tech downturns. Pairing it with funds that hold other sectors, countries or bonds spreads the risk.
