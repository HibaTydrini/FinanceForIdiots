# Shopify Inc. (SHOP.TO / SHOP)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Information Technology (e-commerce software) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (SHOP, CAD) and Nasdaq (SHOP, USD)
Also known as: Shopify, SHOP, SHOP.TO
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Shopify provides "essential internet infrastructure for commerce." It is the software businesses use to run an online store, plus their in-person sales (point of sale), social media and marketplace sales. Merchants range from small one-person shops to large brands. Shopify is one of the largest Canadian companies by stock market value, and its principal office is in Ottawa, Ontario.

Employees: about 7,600 worldwide at the end of 2025.

## How it makes money
- **Subscription solutions**: monthly plan fees merchants pay to use Shopify, plus apps, themes and domain names. Steady, recurring revenue.
- **Merchant solutions**: fees that grow with merchants' sales, mainly payment processing through Shopify Payments, plus lending (Shopify Capital), shipping labels, point-of-sale hardware and advertising. US$8.8 billion in 2025, up 35%. This is now the larger part of the business.

2025 results:
- Total revenue: US$11.6 billion, up 30%.
- Gross merchandise volume (GMV, the total value of goods sold on Shopify): US$378.4 billion, up 29%.
- Free cash flow: about US$2 billion. Shopify also launched a US$2 billion share buyback.
- Dividend: none. Shopify says it does not expect to pay cash dividends in the foreseeable future.

## What moves the stock
- Online shopping growth and consumer spending. When people spend less, merchants sell less and Shopify earns less.
- Revenue growth rate versus expectations. Shopify is valued as a fast-growing company, so even small slowdowns can cause big price drops.
- Interest rates. Fast-growing tech stocks tend to fall when rates rise.
- Competition from Amazon and other e-commerce platforms.

## Main risks (from its annual report, in plain words)
1. **Keeping and growing merchants.** Growth may slow if fewer new merchants join or existing ones leave.
2. **Competition** from larger companies, new entrants and merchants building their own tools.
3. **Security breaches or outages** could hurt merchants, shoppers and Shopify's reputation.
4. **Keeping up with technology**, including AI tools. If Shopify falls behind, merchants may switch.
5. **The economy.** Downturns, inflation and tariffs reduce spending by merchants and shoppers.
6. **Laws and regulation** on privacy, payments, lending and taxes could raise costs.

## What this means for a beginner
Shopify is a successful, fast-growing Canadian tech company, but fast-growing tech stocks tend to have very large price swings. Because it pays no dividend, all of your return depends on the share price going up. If one stock like Shopify is a large part of your account, a single bad year for that company can hurt your whole account a lot. Shopify is also one of the largest holdings in Canadian index funds such as XIU (about 6.5% as of October 2026), so you may already own some through funds.
