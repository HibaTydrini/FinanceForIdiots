# iShares 20+ Year Treasury Bond ETF (TLT)

Type: Fund (ETF, bonds) | Focus: long-term U.S. government bonds (20+ years to maturity) | Country: United States | Trades on: U.S. stock exchange (USD)
Also known as: TLT, iShares 20+ Year Treasury
As of: November 12–13, 2026 (fund page)

## What it is
TLT holds **U.S. Treasury bonds**, loans to the U.S. government, that have more than 20 years left before they are repaid. It tracks the ICE US Treasury 20+ Year Bond Index and launched July 22, 2002.

U.S. Treasuries are considered to have very low **credit risk** (the U.S. government is very likely to pay). But because these bonds are so long-term, TLT has very high **interest rate risk**.

## Key facts (as of Nov 2026)
- Holdings: 47 bonds.
- Expense ratio: 0.15% per year.
- Net assets: about US$46.2 billion.
- Average maturity: 26.03 years.
- Effective duration: 14.75 years.
- 30-day SEC yield: 5.40%. 12-month trailing yield: 4.90%.

## Why it can fall a lot (duration)
Duration estimates how much a bond fund's price changes when interest rates move. With a duration of about 14.75 years, a 1 percentage point rise in long-term rates would lower TLT's price by roughly 15%. For comparison, a broad bond fund like AGG has a duration of about 5.7 years. This is why long-term bond funds fell sharply when interest rates rose quickly in 2022, and why many people who bought TLT before then are still well below their purchase price.

The flip side: when long-term rates fall, TLT can rise a lot.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Very high interest rate risk.** Price swings can be as big as a stock fund's.
2. **Inflation.** Rising inflation usually pushes long-term rates up.
3. **Currency risk for Canadians.** Priced in U.S. dollars.

## What this means for a beginner
"Government bonds" sounds safe, and TLT is very safe in the sense that the U.S. government will almost certainly pay. But its *price* can still drop a lot because of interest rates. If you want bonds to steady your account, short- or medium-term bond funds (like ZST, XBB or AGG) move much less. Long-term bonds like TLT are better understood as a bet on interest rates falling.
