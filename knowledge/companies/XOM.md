# Exxon Mobil Corporation (XOM)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Energy (integrated oil and gas) | Country: United States | Trades on: NYSE (USD)
Also known as: ExxonMobil, Exxon, XOM
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
ExxonMobil is one of the world's largest publicly traded oil and gas companies. It is "integrated," meaning it does every step: finding and producing oil and gas, refining it into fuels, and making chemicals and specialty products such as lubricants. It also has a Low Carbon Solutions division for lower-emission businesses.

Its business segments:
- **Upstream**: exploring for and producing oil and natural gas. 2025 earnings: US$21.4 billion.
- **Energy Products**: refining crude oil into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel. US$7.4 billion.
- **Chemical Products**: plastics and other petrochemicals. US$0.8 billion.
- **Specialty Products**: lubricants and other specialty items. US$2.9 billion.

## How it makes money
It sells oil, natural gas, fuels and chemicals around the world. Profits depend heavily on oil and gas prices and on refining margins.

2025 results:
- Total revenues and other income: about US$332.2 billion.
- Earnings (profit): US$28.8 billion, down from US$33.7 billion in 2024, mainly because oil prices were lower.
- Production: 4.7 million barrels of oil equivalent per day, its highest in more than 40 years.
- Cash flow from operations: about US$52.0 billion.
- Returned to shareholders: US$37.2 billion (US$17.2 billion in dividends plus US$20.0 billion in share buybacks).
- Dividend: ExxonMobil has grown its annual dividend per share for 43 consecutive years.

## What moves the stock
- **Oil and natural gas prices**: the biggest driver.
- Refining margins (the gap between crude oil costs and fuel prices).
- Global economic growth and geopolitical events (wars, sanctions, OPEC decisions).
- Climate policy and the long-term outlook for oil demand.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Commodity prices.** When oil and gas prices fall, earnings fall. 2025 profit dropped from 2024 for this reason.
2. **Climate regulation and the energy transition.** Stricter rules, carbon pricing or faster adoption of electric vehicles could reduce long-term demand and raise costs.
3. **Geopolitics.** Exxon operates in many countries and can be hit by conflicts, sanctions or government actions.
4. **Large, long projects** can run over budget or be delayed.
5. **Environmental and legal risks**: spills, accidents and climate-related lawsuits.

## What this means for a beginner
ExxonMobil is a huge, established company with a very long dividend record, so many people own it for income. But like all energy companies, its results move with oil prices, which are hard to predict. If you also own other energy stocks (like Canadian Natural or Enbridge), much of your account depends on the same factor. If you care about ESG or climate, look at its sustainability reporting and decide for yourself. ExxonMobil is held inside broad funds like SPY, but is excluded from fossil-fuel-screened funds like ESGV.
