# Enbridge Inc. (ENB.TO / ENB)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Energy (pipelines and gas utilities, "midstream") | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (ENB, CAD) and NYSE (ENB, USD)
Also known as: Enbridge, ENB
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Enbridge is one of North America's largest energy infrastructure companies. Unlike an oil producer, it mostly does not sell oil or gas itself. It **moves and delivers** energy through pipelines and gas utilities, and charges fees for it, a bit like a toll road. Headquarters: Calgary, Alberta.

Its four businesses (2025 adjusted EBITDA, a measure of operating cash earnings, in Canadian dollars):
- **Liquids Pipelines**: carries crude oil from Western Canada to refineries in Canada and the U.S. Its Mainline system moved about 3.1 million barrels per day in 2025. C$9.7 billion.
- **Gas Transmission**: large natural gas pipelines in the U.S. and Canada. C$5.4 billion.
- **Gas Distribution and Storage**: natural gas utilities that deliver gas to homes and businesses, including Enbridge Gas Ontario and U.S. utilities in Ohio, Utah and North Carolina. C$4.1 billion.
- **Renewable Power Generation**: wind and solar projects. C$0.7 billion.

## How it makes money
Mostly through long-term contracts and regulated rates for using its pipelines and utilities. This makes its cash flow steadier than an oil producer's, because it earns fees on the volume it moves even when oil prices change.

2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Adjusted EBITDA: about C$20.0 billion, up 7%.
- GAAP earnings to common shareholders: about C$7.1 billion (C$3.23 per share).
- Distributable cash flow: about C$12.5 billion.
- Dividend: raised 3% to C$0.97 per share per quarter (C$3.88 per year) starting March 2026. This is its 31st consecutive annual increase.
- Debt: debt-to-EBITDA of 4.8x, within its 4.5x to 5.0x target.

## What moves the stock
- **Interest rates.** Enbridge carries a lot of debt and is often bought for its dividend, so it tends to fall when interest rates rise and rise when they fall.
- Regulatory decisions on utility rates and pipeline projects.
- Volumes of oil and gas moving through its system.
- Large new projects and how they are financed.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **High debt and interest rates.** Higher rates raise borrowing costs and make its dividend less attractive compared with bonds.
2. **Regulation and permits.** Rate decisions and pipeline approvals can be delayed, denied or challenged.
3. **Energy transition.** Long-term demand for oil and gas pipelines could decline.
4. **Environmental and safety risks**: pipeline leaks or spills can be costly and damage reputation.
5. **Project execution**: big projects can run over budget.

## What this means for a beginner
Enbridge is known in Canada as a dividend stock with steady, contract-based cash flow. It is usually less tied to oil prices than oil producers, but it is still an energy company, it is sensitive to interest rates and it carries a lot of debt. A high dividend is not a guarantee: dividends can be cut if a company runs into trouble. If you care about ESG, note that most of Enbridge's business is fossil fuel infrastructure, and look at its sustainability reports to decide whether it fits your values. ENB is also a top-10 holding in XIU.
