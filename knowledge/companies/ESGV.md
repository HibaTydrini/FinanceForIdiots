# Vanguard ESG U.S. Stock ETF (ESGV)

Type: Fund (ETF, stocks, ESG-screened) | Focus: U.S. companies of all sizes, excluding certain industries | Country: United States | Trades on: U.S. stock exchange (USD)
Also known as: ESGV, Vanguard ESG U.S. Stock
As of: November 2026 (Vanguard ETF list)

## What it is
ESGV is a U.S. stock fund for people who want to avoid certain industries for ethical or environmental reasons. ESG stands for Environmental, Social and Governance. It tracks the **FTSE US All Cap Choice Index**, a size-weighted index of large, mid-size and small U.S. companies, after removing companies that fail its screens. Vanguard launched it in 2018.

## What it excludes
The index provider (FTSE) removes companies involved in, or earning a set amount of revenue from:
- **Fossil fuels**: coal, oil and gas
- Nuclear power
- Weapons: conventional military weapons, civilian firearms, nuclear weapons, chemical and biological weapons, cluster munitions, landmines
- Tobacco, alcohol, cannabis, gambling and adult entertainment

It also removes companies that don't meet certain standards on labour, human rights, environment, anti-corruption and diversity.

## Key facts
- Expense ratio: 0.09% per year (about US$0.90 per US$1,000 invested).
- Holds a very large number of U.S. companies of all sizes.

## What moves it
- The overall U.S. stock market, especially large companies, which dominate size-weighted indexes.
- Because energy stocks are excluded, it can do better than the broad market when oil companies fall, and worse when they rally.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Stock market risk.** It is 100% stocks and can drop a lot in a bad year.
2. **Different from the market.** Vanguard notes ESG-screened funds "may perform differently than the broad market" because some companies are excluded.
3. **Tech-heavy.** Removing energy and other industries leaves relatively more weight in technology.
4. **Screens may not match your values exactly.** FTSE decides what counts, so read the rules.
5. **Currency risk for Canadians.** Priced in U.S. dollars.

## What this means for a beginner
If you care about ESG, a screened fund like ESGV is an easy way to invest broadly while avoiding fossil fuels and other excluded industries. It still behaves mostly like the U.S. stock market. One thing to check: owning an ESG fund doesn't make the rest of your account ESG. If you also hold oil and gas companies directly (like Canadian Natural, Exxon Mobil or Enbridge), those are exactly the kinds of companies this fund screens out.
