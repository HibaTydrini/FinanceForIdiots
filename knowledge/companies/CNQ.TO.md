# Canadian Natural Resources Limited (CNQ.TO / CNQ)

Type: Company (stock) | Sector: Energy (oil and natural gas production) | Country: Canada | Trades on: Toronto Stock Exchange (CNQ, CAD) and NYSE (CNQ, USD)
Also known as: Canadian Natural, CNRL, CNQ
As of: fiscal year 2025 (ended December 31, 2025)

## What it does
Canadian Natural is one of Canada's largest oil and natural gas producers. It digs and pumps oil and gas out of the ground and sells it. Its main operations are in Western Canada, with smaller ones in the U.K. North Sea and Offshore Africa. Headquarters: Calgary, Alberta.

Its main businesses:
- **Oil sands mining and upgrading**: mining oil sands in northern Alberta and upgrading them into synthetic crude oil (SCO). A record 565,102 barrels per day in 2025.
- **Thermal in situ**: pumping steam underground to extract heavy oil. A record 275,086 barrels per day in 2025.
- **Conventional oil and natural gas** in Western Canada. A record 2,538 million cubic feet per day of North American natural gas in 2025.

## How it makes money
It sells crude oil, natural gas and natural gas liquids. Its revenue and profit depend mostly on **oil and gas prices**, which it does not control.

2025 results (Canadian dollars):
- Production: a record of about 1,571,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day, up 15%, partly from acquisitions.
- Net earnings: about C$10.8 billion.
- Adjusted funds flow (cash generated by operations): about C$15.5 billion.
- Reserves: about 31 years of proved reserves at current production.
- Dividend: raised to C$0.625 per share per quarter. 2026 is its 26th consecutive year of dividend increases.

## What moves the stock
- **Oil prices** above all, plus natural gas prices.
- The price gap between Canadian heavy oil and U.S. oil, and pipeline capacity to get Canadian oil to market.
- Government policy on carbon pricing and methane emissions. Canadian Natural deferred an oil sands expansion (Jackpine) because those rules were not finalized.
- The Canadian dollar, since oil is priced in U.S. dollars.

## Main risks (in plain words)
1. **Oil and gas prices can swing a lot** with the global economy, OPEC decisions and wars. Profits rise and fall with them.
2. **Climate policy and the energy transition.** Stricter emissions rules or carbon prices raise costs, and lower long-term demand for oil would hurt.
3. **Environmental, social and governance (ESG) concerns.** Oil sands production is energy-intensive. The company budgets for carbon capture (C$125 million for 2026) and is testing lower-emission extraction methods, but some investors avoid the oil and gas sector entirely.
4. **Pipeline and transportation limits** can lower the price Canadian producers receive.
5. **Large projects** can run over budget or be delayed.

## What this means for a beginner
Canadian Natural is a large, profitable company with a long record of raising its dividend, which is why many Canadian income investors own it. But it is an energy company, and its results move with oil prices. Owning several energy companies at once (for example CNQ, Exxon Mobil and Enbridge) means much of your account rises and falls with the same thing. If you care about ESG or climate, you may want to read the company's sustainability reports and decide whether it fits your values. The numbers alone can't tell you that. CNQ is also a top-10 holding in Canadian index funds such as XIU.
